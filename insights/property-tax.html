Decoding the New Tax Landscape for Real Estate Investors

The purchase price is not the cost. Stamp duty, capital gains, withholding and state charges decide whether a deal is still the deal you thought it was.

Property transactions in Nigeria fail quietly: not at the viewing, but at the point when title, tax clearance and the true closing cost are put on one page. The work is to put that page together early.

Map the taxes before you negotiate

Who pays stamp duty, who bears capital gains, and what the state land registry will require should be agreed in the offer, not discovered in the deed. A clean allocation in the contract prevents the last-week argument that kills closings.

Title and tax history travel together

A beautiful building with an incomplete tax history is not a completed asset. Ask for receipts, consent status and any outstanding charges as part of diligence, not as a favour after heads of terms.

Structure the buyer with the hold in mind

Buying in a personal name, a special-purpose company or a family vehicle changes later sale, inheritance and banking. The cheapest registration today is not always the cheapest exit in five years.

If a purchase or disposal is in front of you, we can walk the tax and the papers with you.
