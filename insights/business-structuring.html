Navigating Tomorrow’s Tax Landscape

Preparing for Nigeria’s 2026 tax reforms with insight and confidence — the same briefing published on highlightconsultingservices.com.

Nigeria’s tax environment is entering a defining phase. Discussions around a 2026 tax reform framework are aimed at raising non-oil revenue, improving transparency, aligning with global standards and making it easier to do business.

Why this conversation matters now

Waiting until the reforms are official can leave a business exposed. The pressures are already visible: the need for non-oil revenue, informal-market leakage, digital tax administration, BEPS alignment, and the need to support SMEs while cutting evasion.

Areas to watch

Signals point to possible change in personal income tax bands and PAYE monitoring, company income tax thresholds and incentives, VAT rates and digital services, capital gains and wealth, and mandatory e-filing with automated penalties.

Who is most affected

SMEs and startups, multinationals, gig workers, real-estate investors, expatriates, high-income earners and import-dependent manufacturers. HCSL helps clients plan, interpret and stay audit-ready before enforcement tightens.
